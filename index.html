CHAPTER01
THE POSTCODE PROBLEM

Where are all the supermarkets?

Location totals tell one story. Dividing by population tells another. Four spatial views reveal why the biggest states are not automatically the best supplied.

MAP ROOM ◉ ◉ ◉
THE DESIGN QUESTION: ABSOLUTE OR RELATIVE?

A population of more than eight million gives NSW far more stores than Tasmania, even when the proportion per resident is lower. The first two maps therefore answer different questions. The cartogram and tile view then deliberately reduce geographic size so the quantitative differences are easier to compare.

Count the stores, then count the people

THE POINT · A RATE, NOT A TOTAL

Tasmania records 8.44 combined Woolworths and Coles locations for every 100,000 residents, compared with 6.99 in NSW. A state can have fewer stores overall yet a higher number relative to its population.

HOW TO READ THE EVIDENCE

A small glossary for a big grocery story.

These definitions explain the decisions behind the charts. The point is to understand the data, not memorise technical vocabulary.

Absolute vs relative

A total counts locations. A per-capita rate divides the total by population: stores ÷ residents × 100,000. Both are useful, but answer different questions.

Area, colour & position

The larger a proportional circle or treemap rectangle, the more stores it represents. A sequential light-to-dark colour scale encodes smaller to larger numeric values.

Store share vs sales share

Store share counts a retailer’s locations out of the two sampled chains. Sales share estimates the portion of all take-home grocery dollars associated with a participant.

Percentage vs percentage points

A rise from 5% to 8% is +3 percentage points, not +3% growth. The diverging bars use percentage points.

A map is not a travel-time model

State rates and approximate symbols do not tell us how far a shopper lives from a store, whether it has accessible transport, or what the food costs there.

Read the exact value

Hover on a chart to see figures. On the charts with clickable legends, select a retailer or state to highlight it; click again or reset the legend to return to the broader view.

THE RECEIPT / WHAT WE LEARNED

Big footprint.
Different stories.

There is no single statistic that explains supermarket shopping. Store counts map presence, sales shares describe market concentration, and food turnover captures broader spending in current dollars.

01

Geography: more stores overall does not always mean more stores per resident.

02

Competition: Woolworths and Coles account for 57.3% of estimated national take-home grocery sales in FY2022–23.

03

Spending: ABS national food turnover grew in nominal dollars, but that doesn't prove Australians bought more food.
